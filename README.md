# FIREBEARD'S SHIP CURRIES FOR LAND DWELLERS

![Front cover illustration](firebeards_ship_curries_front_cover.png)

### A Galley Guide to Lentils, Spice, Fire and Feeding a Hungry Crew

You need a pot, a spoon, some lentils and enough nerve to let an onion take its time.

This is not a crate of unrelated recipes. It is one dependable curry method, followed by fifteen directions in which to sail it: gentle, hot, coconut-rich, dark and savoury, green and bright, mustardy, peppery, creamy, tangy and more. Learn the method once; use the ingredient guides to change the destination.

All quantities serve 4 with rice or flatbread.

---

# HOW THIS BOOK WORKS

Every curry is cooked in the same seven stages. Read the method once. Then choose a curry from the guides and add the listed ingredients at each stage.

## The Seven Stages

| Stage | What happens | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Ready the lentils | Rinse and measure liquid | Makes the cooking predictable |
| 2. Wake the spices | Fry whole spices in fat | Releases deep aroma |
| 3. Build the foundation | Cook onion, shallot or another base | Creates sweetness and body |
| 4. Add the sharp things | Cook garlic, ginger and fresh chilli | Adds punch without burning them |
| 5. Bloom the powders | Briefly fry ground spices | Removes rawness and opens flavour |
| 6. Simmer the curry | Add lentils and liquid; cook until right | Brings everything together |
| 7. Finish and balance | Add acid, herbs, rich fat or tadka | Makes it taste alive rather than merely cooked |

## The Pantry Base

Keep these aboard and nearly every curry in this book is possible:

- red lentils
- onions
- garlic and ginger
- neutral oil, ghee or butter
- cumin seeds, ground coriander, turmeric and chilli powder
- tinned tomatoes
- coconut milk
- salt, sugar and lemons or limes

The more unusual things—black cardamom, curry leaves, fenugreek, tamarind—are optional upgrades, not admission tickets.

# THE ONE METHOD

## A Simple Lentil Curry Base

Use a wide saucepan or deep frying pan with a lid. Have the ingredients for your chosen curry measured before the oil goes in. Spices wait for nobody.

### Stage 1 — Ready the lentils

Rinse **250 g red lentils** in a sieve until the water runs mostly clear. Set them aside with **750 ml water or light stock**.

Red lentils are the default because they cook in about 20–25 minutes and make their own creamy sauce. For brown, green or black lentils, see [Choosing Your Lentil](#choosing-your-lentil) below.

### Stage 2 — Wake the spices

Heat **2 tablespoons fat** over medium heat. Add the whole spices listed for your chosen curry. Cook only until they smell fragrant—usually 20 to 40 seconds.

If cumin goes from nutty to acrid, or a dried chilli starts smoking, the pan is too hot. Take it off the heat for a moment. Wake the spice; do not cremate it.

### Stage 3 — Build the foundation

Add the onion, shallot or other foundation. Add a pinch of salt and cook at the pace specified in the guide.

- **Soft and pale:** 5–8 minutes. Gentle, sweet, mild.
- **Golden:** 10–15 minutes. Rounder and richer.
- **Deep brown:** 20–30 minutes. Dark, savoury and powerful. Add splashes of water if it catches.

### Stage 4 — Add the sharp things

Add the garlic, ginger and fresh chilli in the guide. Stir for 30–60 seconds, just until the raw smell softens. Garlic burns fast; it is not a test of character.

### Stage 5 — Bloom the powders

Add the ground spices. Stir for 15–30 seconds. If the pan is dry, add a splash of water, tomato or stock immediately; ground spices burn even faster than garlic.

### Stage 6 — Simmer the curry

Add any tomatoes or other bulk ingredients called for, then the lentils and liquid. Bring to a boil, reduce to a gentle simmer, and stir every few minutes so the lentils do not weld themselves to the pan.

Simmer uncovered for **20–25 minutes** until the red lentils are tender. Add a little more hot water if it becomes thicker than you want. A curry should be spoonable, not mortar.

### Stage 7 — Finish and balance

Turn off the heat. Stir through the finishing ingredients in the guide, then taste.

- Tastes flat? Add salt.
- Tastes heavy? Add acid—lime, lemon, tamarind or a little vinegar.
- Tastes sharp or bitter? Add a small pinch of sugar or an extra knob of butter/coconut cream.
- Tastes thin? Simmer it longer, uncovered.
- Tastes dull despite all that? It probably needs both salt and acid.

Rest for 5 minutes before serving. Curry improves when it has a moment to stop shouting.

# THE CURRY GUIDES

Choose one guide. Follow the master method, using the ingredients listed at each stage. These are not additions to one another: each is a complete route from pot to bowl.

## CURRY I — THE CALM SEA

### Gentle Butter Dal

Warm, soft and rich. This is depth without an argument.

| Stage | Add this | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Lentils | 250 g red lentils; 750 ml water or light stock | The standard base |
| 2. Wake | 1½ tbsp ghee or butter; 1 tsp cumin seeds | Keep heat medium-low |
| 3. Foundation | 1 small onion, finely chopped; pinch of salt | Cook soft and pale, 6–8 min |
| 4. Sharp things | 2 garlic cloves, finely grated; 1 tsp grated ginger | Cook 30 sec |
| 5. Powders | 1 tsp turmeric; 1 tsp ground coriander; pinch mild chilli powder | Optional chilli is for warmth, not punishment |
| 6. Simmer | The lentils and liquid from stage 1 | 20–25 min, until creamy |
| 7. Finish | 1 tbsp butter; ½ tsp garam masala; 1 tbsp lemon juice; coriander leaves | Add the lemon a little at a time |

**Optional finishing tadka:** melt 1 tablespoon butter, fry ½ teaspoon cumin seeds and a sliced garlic clove until fragrant, then pour it over the bowl just before serving.

## CURRY II — BROADSIDE

### Firebeard's Hot Tomato Lentils

Sharp, red and unapologetic. The aim is not merely heat: it is heat that keeps you coming back.

| Stage | Add this | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Lentils | 250 g red lentils; 650 ml water or stock | Tomatoes add the remaining liquid |
| 2. Wake | 2 tbsp oil; 1 tsp cumin seeds; 1 dried red chilli | Remove dried chilli later if you prefer less heat |
| 3. Foundation | 1 large onion, finely chopped; pinch of salt | Cook golden to deep gold, 12–15 min |
| 4. Sharp things | 3 garlic cloves; 1 tbsp grated ginger; 1 fresh red chilli, sliced | Save some fresh chilli for the end if desired |
| 5. Powders | 1 tsp turmeric; 2 tsp ground coriander; 1 tsp ground cumin; ½–1 tsp chilli powder | Use the lower amount first; you can add heat, not remove it |
| 6. Simmer | 1 x 400 g tin chopped tomatoes; lentils and liquid; 1 tsp sugar | Cook tomatoes for 3 min before adding lentils; simmer 20–25 min |
| 7. Finish | 1 tsp tamarind or 1 tbsp lemon juice; ½ tsp garam masala; coriander; salt | Add sugar only if the tomatoes taste harsh |

**Firebeard's rule:** chilli belongs in layers. A dried chilli gives bass notes, powder gives body, and fresh chilli gives a bright sting. Use one, two or all three according to the crew.

## CURRY III — TRADE WINDS

### Coconut Lentil Curry

Creamy, aromatic and lightly sweet, with lime keeping the whole ship upright.

| Stage | Add this | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Lentils | 250 g red lentils; 400 ml water or stock | Coconut milk comes later |
| 2. Wake | 1 tbsp coconut oil; 1 tsp mustard seeds; 8–10 curry leaves, if available | Stand back when mustard seeds begin to pop |
| 3. Foundation | 1 small onion or 2 shallots, finely chopped; pinch of salt | Cook soft and pale, 6–8 min |
| 4. Sharp things | 2 garlic cloves; 1 tbsp grated ginger; 1 green chilli, sliced | Leave the seeds in for more heat |
| 5. Powders | 1 tsp turmeric; 2 tsp ground coriander; ½ tsp ground cumin | Do not overdo the spices; coconut needs room |
| 6. Simmer | Lentils and water/stock; 1 x 400 ml tin full-fat coconut milk | Simmer 18–22 min; keep it gentle after coconut goes in |
| 7. Finish | Zest and juice of 1 lime; coriander; salt | Lime is not optional in spirit: it stops richness becoming cloying |

For a looser, soupier curry add 100–200 ml extra water. For a richer one, finish with a spoonful of coconut cream.

## CURRY IV — THE BLACK HULL

### Dark Roasted Lentil Curry

Earthy, dark and almost meaty. This one earns its depth through time.

| Stage | Add this | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Lentils | 250 g brown or black lentils; 1 litre water or rich vegetable stock | Soak black lentils overnight if possible; see lentil guide |
| 2. Wake | 2 tbsp oil or ghee; 1 tsp cumin seeds; 1 black cardamom pod, lightly crushed; 1 bay leaf | Black cardamom is smoky and powerful; use one pod |
| 3. Foundation | 2 large onions, finely chopped; pinch of salt | Cook deep brown, 20–30 min; add water by the spoonful if needed |
| 4. Sharp things | 3 garlic cloves; 1 tbsp grated ginger | Cook 45 sec |
| 5. Powders | 2 tsp ground coriander; 1 tsp ground cumin; ½ tsp turmeric; ½ tsp chilli powder | Keep the chilli restrained; darkness is the point |
| 6. Simmer | 1 x 400 g tin chopped tomatoes; lentils and stock; 1 tsp tomato purée | Cook tomatoes until thick before adding lentils. Simmer covered 35–50 min, until tender; add water as needed |
| 7. Finish | 1 tsp garam masala; 1 tsp tamarind or 1 tbsp lemon juice; 1 tbsp butter; coriander | A tiny pinch of sugar can round a bitter edge |

Remove the bay leaf and black cardamom before serving if you find them. They have done their job and need not be chewed as a reward.

## CURRY V — GREEN WATER

### Fresh Herb and Lime Lentils

Bright, sharp and alive. It is deliberately not a heavy curry.

| Stage | Add this | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Lentils | 250 g red lentils; 700 ml water or light stock | Keep the finished curry a little looser |
| 2. Wake | 2 tbsp neutral oil; 1 tsp cumin seeds | A pinch of fennel seed is optional |
| 3. Foundation | 1 small onion or 2 shallots, finely chopped; pinch of salt | Cook soft, not brown, 5–7 min |
| 4. Sharp things | 2 garlic cloves; 1 tbsp grated ginger; 1–2 green chillies, sliced | Add half the chilli now, save half for finishing |
| 5. Powders | ½ tsp turmeric; 1 tsp ground coriander | Less powder means the herbs remain in command |
| 6. Simmer | Lentils and liquid | Simmer 18–22 min, adding a splash of water if needed |
| 7. Finish | Large handful coriander, chopped; small handful mint, chopped; zest and juice of 1 lime; remaining green chilli; 1 tbsp olive oil or ghee | Stir herbs in off the heat so they stay fresh |

Serve with rice, flatbread and something cold to drink. This is the curry for warm weather and open windows.

# CHOOSING YOUR LENTIL

The staged method stays the same. Only the liquid and simmering time change.

| Lentil | Texture | Rinse/soak | Approx. liquid for 250 g | Simmering time | Best routes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Red | Soft, creamy, dissolving | Rinse only | 700–800 ml | 20–25 min | Calm Sea, Broadside, Trade Winds, Green Water |
| Yellow split lentils | Gentle and buttery | Rinse; soak 30 min if time allows | 800–900 ml | 30–40 min | Calm Sea, Trade Winds |
| Green | Firm and earthy | Rinse only | 900 ml–1 litre | 35–45 min | Black Hull, Green Water |
| Brown | Robust, savoury | Rinse only | 900 ml–1 litre | 35–50 min | Black Hull, Broadside |
| Black (whole urad) | Dense and rich | Rinse; soak overnight if possible | 1 litre, plus more if needed | 60–90 min | Black Hull |

Cooking times vary with the age of the lentils. If they are still hard, they are not done; give them more simmering water and more time. Add acid only near the end with firm lentils, since it can slow their softening.

# THE FIREBEARD TEST

Before serving, ask the pot these questions:

- **Does it need salt?** Salt makes the flavours already present speak up.
- **Does it need acid?** Lemon, lime, tamarind or tomato wakes a heavy curry.
- **Do the onions need longer?** Pale onions are not wrong, but they make a different curry from brown ones.
- **Is it too hot or bitter?** Add fat, a little sugar, and time—not a panic attack.
- **Is it too thick?** Add hot water a splash at a time.
- **Is it too thin?** Simmer uncovered.
- **Is it done?** If the lentils are tender, the seasoning balanced and you want a second spoonful, stop touching it.

At that point you no longer need a recipe. Which is the entire point.
